WebP vs WebP 2: Key Differences and Features

WebP has become a standard format for efficient image delivery across the internet, but Google developed WebP 2 as an experimental successor to address modern display and bandwidth demands. This article explores the primary technical and architectural differences separating WebP 2 from the original WebP specification, highlighting improvements in compression efficiency, color representation, resolution thresholds, and parallel processing.

Advanced Compression and Quality Efficiency

The original WebP format is based on the VP8 video codec's intra-frame prediction technology. While efficient, its compression mechanisms are over a decade old. WebP 2 implements modernized transform coding, partition trees, and Asymmetric Numeral Systems (ANS) entropy coding. These architectural upgrades allow WebP 2 to achieve roughly 30% better compression than the original WebP format at equivalent visual quality levels, bringing it on par with modern codecs like AVIF.

High Dynamic Range and Expanded Bit Depth

A major limitation of the original WebP standard is its strict adherence to an 8-bit color depth per channel. This limitation often causes banding artifacts in gradients and restricts the format's ability to display High Dynamic Range (HDR) content. WebP 2 introduces native support for 10-bit color depth, wide color gamuts, and HDR imagery. This ensures smoother tonal transitions, accurate preservation of vibrant colors, and full compatibility with modern display panels.

Significantly Larger Resolution Limits

Original WebP images are constrained by a maximum dimension limit of 16,383 × 16,383 pixels. While sufficient for standard web graphics, this restriction poses severe challenges for ultra-high-resolution photography, scientific imaging, digital maps, and large panoramas. WebP 2 eliminates this bottleneck by expanding the theoretical limit to billions of pixels along both the X and Y axes, making it suitable for modern high-resolution applications.

Improved Alpha and Lossless Processing

While original WebP supported both lossy and lossless modes, its handling of alpha channels (transparency) and mixed-mode content was often computationally expensive or suboptimal in size. WebP 2 revamps lossless compression with better modeling and prediction tools. It also handles transparency more efficiently by tailoring the compression of the alpha channel directly alongside color components rather than treating it primarily as an appended layer.

Superior Progressive Decoding

WebP 2 features built-in support for progressive decoding, allowing low-fidelity approximations of images to appear immediately while higher-frequency details load gradually over the network. Although original WebP has an incremental decoding mechanism, it lacks the sophisticated multi-pass progressive rendering that formats like progressive JPEG or WebP 2 offer to improve perceived loading speeds on slow connections.

Multi-Threading and Modern Hardware Utilization

Decoding original WebP can sometimes become a CPU bottleneck on mobile hardware because of its sequential nature. WebP 2 was built from the ground up to utilize modern multi-core architectures. It incorporates spatial tiling, which breaks large images into independent blocks that can be encoded and decoded in parallel across multiple CPU threads, resulting in faster processing times and lower power consumption.
