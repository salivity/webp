Can Adversarial Attacks Survive WebP Compression?

This article explores whether adversarial visual perturbations can survive WebP lossy compression, functioning effectively against machine learning models. While standard, unoptimized adversarial noise is typically degraded or neutralized by the quantization and transform coding stages of WebP, carefully crafted perturbations can indeed persist. By incorporating compression-aware optimization techniques—such as differentiable compression approximations and low-frequency perturbation mapping—attackers can generate robust adversarial examples that maintain their potency even after lossy WebP encoding.

The Impact of WebP Lossy Compression on Perturbations

WebP lossy compression is based on the VP8 video codec's intra-frame encoding. It processes images by partitioning them into macroblocks, applying discrete cosine transforms (DCT) or Walsh-Hadamard transforms, and quantizing the resulting frequency coefficients.

Most conventional adversarial attacks, such as basic Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) or standard Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), introduce high-frequency, low-magnitude noise across pixel values. Because lossy WebP compression specifically discards high-frequency details that are less noticeable to human eyes, standard adversarial perturbations are largely removed or altered during the quantization step. As a result, vanilla adversarial samples often fail to fool downstream vision models after being converted to WebP.

How Adversarial Perturbations Can Be Preserved

Despite the natural filtering effect of WebP compression, adversarial perturbations can be engineered to survive the process through several specialized methodologies:

1. Expectation Over Transformation (EOT)

Attackers can use the Expectation Over Transformation framework to model the compression process during the optimization phase. By optimizing the perturbation over a distribution of randomized transformations—including varying WebP quality factors—the attack becomes resilient to the artifacts introduced by the codec.

2. Differentiable Codec Approximations

A primary challenge in attacking compression pipelines is that quantization is a non-differentiable operation, which blocks gradient flow during backpropagation. To overcome this, researchers use differentiable approximations of the WebP pipeline (such as straight-through estimators or continuous Fourier-based approximations). This allows the attack algorithm to calculate gradients through the compression layer and adjust pixel perturbations accordingly.

3. Targeting Low and Mid Frequencies

Because lossy WebP heavily compresses high-frequency data while preserving lower-frequency structural information, perturbations can be explicitly constrained to lower frequency bands. Aligning adversarial signals with the DCT coefficients that WebP prioritizes ensures that the critical adversarial features pass through the quantization matrix virtually intact.

4. Increasing Perturbation Magnitude

When imperceptibility constraints are relaxed, increasing the overall magnitude of the perturbation (\(\epsilon\)) allows the adversarial signal to dominate the underlying image features. While this makes the modification more visible to humans, it significantly increases the likelihood that the adversarial intent survives aggressive quantization.

Implications for Computer Vision Security

Using WebP lossy compression as an input pre-processing defense is generally considered a form of "gradient masking" or "security through obscurity." While it reliably disrupts weak or compression-agnostic attacks, it does not provide true robustness against an adversary aware of the preprocessing pipeline. Relying solely on lossy format conversion is insufficient for secure computer vision systems; effective defense requires robust model training, such as adversarial training, combined with verified detection frameworks.
