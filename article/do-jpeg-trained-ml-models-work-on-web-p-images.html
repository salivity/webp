Do JPEG Trained ML Models Work on WebP Images?

Computer vision models trained on JPEG images generally perform well on WebP inputs, but they are not always equally effective. While image models process raw pixel tensors rather than compressed file formats, differences in compression algorithms introduce distinct visual artifacts and high-frequency pixel variations. This article explores how lossy compression discrepancies between JPEG and WebP influence model accuracy, where performance degradation typically occurs, and how to mitigate cross-format domain shifts.

The Mechanism: From Encoded Files to RGB Tensors

Deep learning models do not directly ingest JPEG or WebP bitstreams. Before inference, an image is decoded into a numeric multidimensional array (typically an RGB tensor) representing pixel intensities.

Because the model interacts only with the decoded pixel values, any operational difference between JPEG and WebP arises strictly from how each compression standard approximates and alters those values during the lossy encoding process. If a model detects features based on micro-textures or spatial frequencies that change during compression, performance discrepancies will emerge.

Compression Artifact Discrepancies

The difference in how JPEG and WebP handle lossy compression is the primary driver of performance variation:

  • JPEG Compression: Uses the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) across fixed \(8 \times 8\) pixel blocks. At lower to medium quality levels, JPEG introduces high-frequency ringing artifacts, edge noise, and noticeable grid-like block boundaries.
  • WebP Compression: Employs intra-frame predictive coding derived from the VP8 video codec. Rather than strictly relying on independent \(8 \times 8\) blocks, WebP predicts pixel values from neighboring blocks and applies deblocking filters. This results in smoother gradients and softer edges, trading blocky noise for blur.

Because deep convolutional networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs) often exhibit a bias toward high-frequency spatial patterns and local textures, a model trained predominantly on JPEG data learns representations that accommodate or rely on JPEG-specific high-frequency features. When presented with the smoother, prediction-smoothed patches of a WebP image, feature activations can shift unpredictably.

Empirical Effectiveness Across Use Cases

In standard classification tasks (such as ImageNet-style benchmarks), high-quality WebP images (quality factor \(\ge 80\)) cause a negligible performance drop—typically less than 1% when compared to equivalent JPEG inputs.

However, measurable performance drops occur under specific conditions:

  1. Aggressive Compression: At medium-to-low quality settings (quality factor \(< 60\)), WebP aggressively blurs fine textures. While human observers often prefer this blur over JPEG’s harsh blocking, models trained on JPEG frequently misclassify these blurred representations because key edge cues are altered.
  2. Fine-Grained and Pixel-Level Tasks: Tasks such as semantic segmentation, optical character recognition (OCR), medical imaging diagnostics, and defect detection are highly sensitive to subtle edge boundaries. The smoothing behavior of lossy WebP can distort faint boundaries that a JPEG-trained model would otherwise identify.
  3. Out-of-Distribution Robustness: Deep models evaluated against corruptions frequently reveal that models trained on one compression standard exhibit reduced out-of-distribution robustness when tested on another, as the compression artifacts act as subtle covariate shifts.

Ensuring Parity Between Formats

To ensure optimal effectiveness when deploying models to environments where WebP inputs are common:

  • Include Format Augmentation in Training: Augment the training pipeline by dynamically compressing training images using various codecs (including standard JPEG, WebP, and AVIF) and random quality settings. This teaches the network invariant representations rather than format-dependent artifact representations.
  • Match Quality Settings in Production: If images must be converted from WebP to JPEG before inference, ensure the target quality is high to avoid compounding multiple generations of compression artifacts.
  • Prefer Lossless WebP for Texture-Sensitive Tasks: If storage constraints require WebP in specialized pipelines (such as medical imaging or satellite analysis), use lossless WebP to guarantee pixel-identical representations to uncompressed sources.
