WebP vs MozJPEG: Visual Quality at Low Bitrates

This article evaluates whether WebP lossy compression can match or exceed the visual quality of MozJPEG at lower bitrates. It breaks down the underlying compression architectures of both formats, compares their artifact profiles under heavy compression, examines psychovisual performance across different types of imagery, and provides a clear verdict on which encoder delivers superior visual fidelity when bandwidth is strictly constrained.

Compression Architectures: VP8 vs. Advanced DCT

The visual divergence between WebP and MozJPEG stems from their core compression mechanisms. MozJPEG is an optimized discrete cosine transform (DCT) encoder built on the traditional JPEG standard. It achieves high compression efficiency by implementing trellis quantization, progressive scan optimization, and psychovisual rate-distortion profiling without breaking compatibility with standard JPEG decoders.

WebP lossy is derived from the VP8 video codec's intra-frame encoding. It partitions images into macroblocks and predicts pixel values based on adjacent blocks before applying an integer DCT-like transform. Because WebP integrates directional spatial prediction and an in-loop deblocking filter, it processes image data with a fundamentally different approach to handling redundancy.

Artifact Profiles at Low Bitrates

When bitrates drop significantly, the trade-offs between the two formats become visually apparent:

  • MozJPEG Artifacts: When pushed to low bitrates, MozJPEG exhibits high-frequency ringing around sharp edges, color smearing, and visible 8x8 pixel grid blocking. While its trellis quantization smooths non-essential variations to save bytes, the rigid grid structure of the JPEG specification inevitably causes structural grid breakdown once the bit budget is exhausted.
  • WebP Artifacts: WebP avoids grid blocking entirely at low bitrates due to its built-in deblocking filter. Instead, its primary failure mode is aggressive smoothing and posterization. WebP removes fine gradients, eliminates subtle noise, and smudges high-frequency textures into flat, plastic-looking patches.

Perceptual Quality Comparison

At extremely low bitrates—such as under 0.8 to 1.0 bits per pixel (BPP)—WebP generally matches and often exceeds MozJPEG in standardized perceptual tests and subjective human viewing.

Human visual perception is typically more forgiving of moderate edge-preserving blur than of sharp, geometric block edges. In photographic regions with large, smooth gradients (such as skies or skin), WebP maintains visual coherence and clean boundaries where MozJPEG begins to display noticeable checkerboard patterns and mosquito noise around contrasting contours.

However, the nature of the source content influences this outcome:

  • High-Frequency Textures (Foliage, Fabric, Grain): MozJPEG frequently retains more plausible texture than WebP at comparable low bitrates. WebP’s spatial prediction often treats complex grain as discardable noise, resulting in an unnatural "airbrushed" effect, whereas MozJPEG retains the impression of sharpness despite slight compression noise.
  • Mixed Content and Flat Color: For illustrations, UI screenshots, and photos with broad continuous tones, WebP decisively matches and outperforms MozJPEG at lower bitrates by cleanly encoding flat spaces without ringing.

The Verdict

WebP lossy compression can consistently match and often surpass the perceived visual quality of MozJPEG at lower bitrates. By leveraging intra-prediction and automatic deblocking, WebP avoids the visually jarring block boundaries characteristic of MozJPEG under extreme compression, making it the more efficient choice when strict byte budgets take precedence over microscopic surface texture.
