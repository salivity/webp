WebP vs AVIF Compression Efficiency

This article examines the compression efficiency of WebP compared to newer image formats, focusing primarily on AVIF. While WebP served as a significant upgrade over legacy formats like JPEG and PNG, modern formats like AVIF provide substantially better data reduction and image fidelity. Below is a direct comparison of how WebP stacks up against AVIF in terms of file size savings, visual quality at low bitrates, feature support, and performance trade-offs.

Compression Performance: WebP vs. AVIF

AVIF consistently outperforms WebP in compression efficiency. Based on the AV1 video codec, AVIF generally produces files that are 20% to 50% smaller than WebP at equivalent visual quality. While WebP represented an average 25% to 35% reduction over traditional JPEGs, AVIF pushes those boundaries further, making it the superior format for reducing page payload and bandwidth consumption.

Image Quality at Low Bitrates

The difference in efficiency becomes most apparent when images are compressed aggressively to achieve minimal file sizes:

  • WebP Artifacts: At low bitrates, WebP tends to introduce noticeable blurriness, loss of fine texture, and occasional color banding in subtle gradients.
  • AVIF Artifacts: AVIF retains structural details much better under heavy compression. Instead of blocky artifacts or aggressive blurring, it tends to slightly smooth out fine noise, resulting in an image that looks sharper and more natural to the human eye.

Color Depth and Dynamic Range

WebP is fundamentally restricted by its underlying VP8 architecture, limiting it to an 8-bit color depth. This limitation often causes visible banding in high-contrast gradients and skies.

AVIF supports 10-bit and 12-bit color depths along with High Dynamic Range (HDR) and wide color gamuts (such as DCI-P3 and Rec. 2020). This allows AVIF to deliver smoother tonal transitions and superior fidelity without an exponential increase in file size.

Encoding and Decoding Speed

While AVIF wins in file size efficiency, WebP holds an advantage in processing speed:

  • WebP: Highly mature, with extremely fast encoding and decoding speeds. It requires minimal CPU usage, making it efficient for on-the-fly image optimization on servers.
  • AVIF: Highly complex compression algorithms mean encoding times are substantially slower than WebP. While modern hardware decoding is improving on client devices, software-based AVIF decoding can still consume more CPU resources and battery power on older mobile devices.

Practical Adoption

Both WebP and AVIF enjoy widespread support across modern web browsers. However, because of AVIF's higher computational demand during compression, many web architectures utilize a tiered approach: AVIF is delivered to compatible browsers where maximum data savings are critical, with WebP serving as an efficient, lightweight fallback before defaulting to standard JPEG or PNG.
