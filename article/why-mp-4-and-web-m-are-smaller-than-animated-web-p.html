Why MP4 and WebM Are Smaller Than Animated WebP

Animated WebP files are frequently larger than equivalent MP4 or WebM videos due to fundamental differences in how image formats and video codecs compress motion. While WebP adapts a still-image standard to display sequential frames, modern video containers like MP4 (using H.264 or H.265) and WebM (using VP9 or AV1) employ advanced temporal compression algorithms, motion estimation, and chroma subsampling designed specifically for moving pictures. Consequently, video formats eliminate significantly more redundant data across frames, yielding much smaller file sizes at comparable or superior visual quality.

Temporal Compression and Motion Vectors

The primary reason for the size discrepancy is inter-frame prediction. True video codecs do not store every frame as a standalone image. Instead, they divide footage into three types of frames:

  • I-Frames (Keyframes): Complete, independent images.
  • P-Frames (Predicted): Store only the changes relative to the previous frame.
  • B-Frames (Bidirectional): Reference both preceding and subsequent frames to predict changes.

Video codecs use motion vectors to track blocks of pixels moving across the screen. If a camera pans across a background, an MP4 or WebM file simply records the vector direction of the shift rather than redrawing the pixels.

In contrast, animated WebP relies on rudimentary frame-diffing. It can discard identical pixel regions between adjacent frames, but it cannot track motion. If an object moves across the canvas, WebP treats the newly revealed and covered areas as fresh pixel data, resulting in higher data overhead.

Chroma Subsampling

Human vision is significantly more sensitive to variations in brightness (luminance) than in color (chrominance). Video formats take full advantage of this via YUV 4:2:0 chroma subsampling, which reduces the color resolution by 75% without noticeable loss of perceived quality.

Animated WebP supports YUV, but it frequently operates in full-resolution color spaces or less aggressive subsampling modes (like YUV 4:4:4 or RGBA) to preserve graphical sharpness and alpha transparency. Storing this extra chrominance information across every frame inflates the final file size considerably.

Advanced Codec Architectures

WebP's compression technology is derived from VP8, a video codec released in 2008. While efficient for static images compared to legacy JPEG or PNG, its animation implementation lacks over a decade of video compression advancements.

Modern video formats leverage far more sophisticated compression tools:

  • H.264 / AVC: Employs dynamic macroblock partitioning, spatial in-picture prediction, and advanced entropy coding (CABAC).
  • VP9 and AV1 (WebM): Utilize flexible transform block sizes (up to 64x64 or 128x128), directional intra-prediction, and superior in-loop deblocking filters.
  • H.265 / HEVC: Offers highly efficient tree-structured coding units that maximize detail retention at ultra-low bitrates.

Variable Bitrate (VBR) Encoding

Video encoders utilize two-pass and psycho-visual Variable Bitrate (VBR) encoding. This allocates higher bitrates to complex, high-action scenes and drastically reduces the bitrate during static moments. Animated WebP lacks this level of continuous, rate-controlled streaming intelligence; it applies static lossy or lossless compression settings universally across frames, missing opportunities to shave off data during slow or static sequences.
