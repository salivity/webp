Rate-Distortion Optimization in cwebp

This article examines the core rate-distortion optimization (RDO) algorithms implemented within Google’s cwebp encoder for the WebP format. It details how the encoder mathematically balances visual quality against bit budget during lossy compression, specifically covering Lagrangian mode selection, trellis quantization, macroblock segmentation, and how execution varies with compression settings.

The Lagrangian Cost Function

At the foundation of rate-distortion optimization in cwebp is the Lagrangian formulation:

\[J = D + \lambda R\]

In this equation, \(J\) represents the total rate-distortion cost, \(D\) is the visual distortion (typically calculated as Sum of Squared Errors (SSE) or Structural Similarity (SSIM)), \(R\) is the estimated number of bits required to encode the block, and \(\lambda\) (lambda) is the Lagrange multiplier. The encoder calculates \(\lambda\) as a function of the chosen Quantization Parameter (QP). By evaluating candidate decisions against this unified cost metric, cwebp selects the path that minimizes file size for a target visual fidelity.

Intra-Prediction Mode Decision

cwebp operates primarily on the VP8 lossy video codec framework, which utilizes spatial intra-prediction for macroblocks. When determining how to predict a block from adjacent reconstructed pixels, the encoder evaluates:

  • Luma Modes: Four 16x16 macroblock prediction modes (DC, Vertical, Horizontal, TrueMotion) and ten 4x4 sub-block prediction modes.
  • Chroma Modes: Four 8x8 prediction modes applied to U and V color planes.

In fast modes, cwebp uses cheaper heuristics like Sum of Absolute Transformed Differences (SATD) or Sum of Absolute Differences (SAD). However, under full RDO, the encoder performs complete transform, quantization, and entropy coding simulations for candidate modes, picking the mode configuration that produces the lowest Lagrangian cost \(J\).

Trellis Quantization (Rate-Distortion Optimized Quantization)

Transform coefficients resulting from the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and Walsh-Hadamard Transform (WHT) must be quantized into discrete levels. Standard quantization simply rounds coefficients to the nearest integer.

cwebp employs trellis quantization (often referred to as RDOQ). Trellis quantization treats coefficient levels as states in a trellis graph and uses the Viterbi algorithm to determine the optimal rounding path. The algorithm considers:

  • The distortion penalty of shifting a coefficient value up, down, or truncating it to zero.
  • The exact bit cost required by the boolean arithmetic entropy coder to signal that specific coefficient level and run-length.

By systematically evaluating the trade-off between coefficient elimination and reconstruction error, trellis quantization removes high-frequency coefficients that cost excessive bits relative to their perceptual contribution.

Macroblock-to-Segment Mapping Optimization

WebP allows an image to be divided into up to four distinct segments, each configured with its own quantization parameter, loop filter strength, and transform parameters.

cwebp applies an optimization algorithm to cluster macroblocks into these segments based on spatial variance, edge density, and rate-distortion efficiency. Rather than assigning segments statically, the encoder can iteratively adjust segment properties across passes to smooth out flat gradient areas (lowering quantization noise) while dedicating higher bit allocations to complex textures.

Algorithmic Scaling via the Method Switch

The thoroughness of RDO in cwebp is directly governed by the compression method parameter (-m, ranging from 0 to 6):

  • Levels 0–2: Skip full RDO in favor of fast spatial heuristics (SAD/SATD) to maximize encoding speed.
  • Levels 3–4: Introduce RD-based decisions for macroblock modes and selective transform evaluation.
  • Levels 5–6: Enable full RDO, including exhaustive intra-mode search, iterative segment tuning, and complete trellis quantization across all transform blocks.
