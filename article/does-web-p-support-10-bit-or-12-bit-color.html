Does WebP Support 10-Bit or 12-Bit Color?

This article examines whether the WebP image format can natively encode 10-bit or 12-bit color channels. It covers the underlying architecture that governs WebP bit-depth capabilities, explains why higher color depths are not supported in the format's current specification, and outlines alternative modern image formats available for projects requiring deep color or High Dynamic Range (HDR) workflows.

Native Bit-Depth Limitations of WebP

The short answer is no. A WebP file cannot natively encode 10-bit or 12-bit color channels. The WebP specification strictly limits color channel precision to 8 bits per channel.

WebP utilizes two primary compression methods, neither of which supports high bit-depth color:

  • Lossy WebP: Built upon the VP8 video codec's intra-frame encoding system. VP8 is fundamentally constrained to an 8-bit YUV 4:2:0 profile. As a result, lossy WebP cannot exceed 8 bits of data per channel (256 tonal levels per channel).
  • Lossless WebP: Uses a dedicated format developed by Google based on spatial transformations and entropy coding. This pipeline is hardcoded to work strictly with 8-bit ARGB color components.

Even when embedding ICC color profiles for wide color spaces (such as Display P3 or Adobe RGB), the underlying data remains quantized to 8 bits. This limitation often causes visible banding (color stepping) in subtle gradients when trying to reproduce wide color gamuts or HDR imagery.

Why Higher Bit Depths Are Not Supported

WebP was created primarily to optimize web performance by replacing older 8-bit formats like JPEG and PNG with smaller file sizes. When the specification was finalized, consumer displays and web browsers were overwhelmingly limited to standard dynamic range (SDR) and standard sRGB 8-bit outputs. Incorporating 10-bit or 12-bit profiles would have introduced significant decoding overhead and compatibility issues across mobile and desktop hardware at that time.

Alternatives for 10-Bit and 12-Bit Images

If your workflow requires 10-bit, 12-bit, or floating-point color channels to support HDR or prevent gradient banding, you must use alternative modern formats:

  • AVIF (AV1 Image File Format): Derived from the AV1 video standard, AVIF natively supports 8-bit, 10-bit, and 12-bit color depths, along with full HDR signaling and wide browser compatibility.
  • JPEG XL: Designed to replace legacy JPEG, it natively supports high bit depths up to 32 bits per channel, including full float values for professional photography and HDR mastering.
  • HEIC / HEIF: Utilizes the HEVC (H.265) video standard and natively supports 10-bit and 12-bit color, commonly used across Apple ecosystems.
  • PNG: Supports standard 16-bit integer color depth per channel (lossless), though it lacks the modern compression efficiency of AVIF or JPEG XL.
