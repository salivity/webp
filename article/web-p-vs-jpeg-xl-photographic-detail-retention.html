WebP vs JPEG XL: Photographic Detail Retention

When comparing modern image formats for photography, JPEG XL significantly outperforms WebP in preserving fine textures, dynamic range, and edge sharpness, especially at medium-to-high quality settings. While WebP relies on an older video-derived compression scheme that tends to smudge micro-details and wash out subtle gradients, JPEG XL was engineered specifically for high-fidelity static imaging. The result is that JPEG XL can retain complex textures like skin pores, foliage, and fabric at file sizes equal to or smaller than WebP images exhibiting noticeable degradation.

Architectural Foundations and Block Sizes

The disparity in detail retention stems directly from the core architecture of each format. WebP's lossy compression is built on the VP8 video codec, which operates primarily on rigid 16x16 macroblocks. Because it was developed with motion video in mind—where the human eye cannot track minute defects frame-by-frame—WebP uses aggressive in-loop deblocking filters to suppress boundary lines between blocks. In static photography, this causes subtle textures to be treated as noise and smoothed over, producing a distinct "plastic" or smeared appearance.

JPEG XL, by contrast, was designed from the ground up for still photography, utilizing a hybrid architecture based on the Pik and FUIF formats. It uses variable-sized Discrete Cosine Transforms (VarDCT) ranging from 2x2 up to 256x256 pixels, alongside a spline system and edge-preserving modes. This allows the encoder to match its mathematical tools to the specific visual characteristics of different parts of a photo, applying small blocks to preserve chaotic textures and large blocks to smooth surfaces.

High-Frequency Micro-Textures

High-frequency information—such as individual strands of hair, distant tree leaves, sand, and film grain—presents the greatest challenge for lossy compression. Under WebP, high-frequency details are often discarded early in the quantization process to achieve target file sizes. The remaining features are then softened by the format's mandatory deblocking filter, leading to an overall loss of perceived sharpness and micro-contrast.

JPEG XL preserves high frequencies far more effectively due to its use of the Butteraugli psycho-visual error metric. This metric accurately models human vision, distinguishing between imperceptible compression noise and critical photographic detail. JPEG XL can even synthesize fine textures and film grain parametrically, ensuring that the visual texture of an image remains intact without requiring excessive data bits.

Color Depth and Gradient Preservation

Photographic fidelity depends heavily on smooth color gradations in areas such as skies, sunsets, and deep shadows. Lossy WebP is strictly limited to 8-bit color depth and standard dynamic range (SDR), frequently necessitating 4:2:0 chroma subsampling. This restriction inevitably leads to color banding (posterization) and chromatic bleeding along high-contrast edges.

JPEG XL natively supports wide color gamuts, high dynamic range (HDR), and bit depths of up to 32 bits per channel. Because it does not force chroma subsampling and provides superior bit distribution across color channels, JPEG XL reproduces tonal gradations smoothly without banding artifacts, maintaining subtle luminosity transitions that WebP flattens.

Compression Degradation Behavior

When bitrates are constrained, the two formats degrade in fundamentally different ways. As WebP compresses further, it transitions rapidly from slight blurring to severe blockiness and smudged patches. JPEG XL degrades gracefully; rather than introducing distinct square blocks or plastic-looking blurs, its compression artifacts manifest as a subtle, uniform loss of resolution that mimics optical defocusing rather than digital corruption. For professional and high-fidelity photography, JPEG XL is objectively superior in retaining the photographic nuances intended by the photographer.
