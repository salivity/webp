How WebP Handles Black and White Manga and Comics

WebP handles high-contrast black-and-white comic book and manga pages with vastly different results depending on whether lossy or lossless compression is used. While lossy WebP often produces noticeable artifacts around sharp line art and blurs intricate screentones, lossless WebP excels at compressing pure monochrome assets, delivering crisp edges and significantly smaller file sizes than traditional formats like PNG.

The Limits of Lossy WebP for Line Art

High-contrast manga and comic pages typically feature stark transitions between pure black (#000000) and pure white (#FFFFFF). Lossy WebP relies on predictive spatial transform algorithms and macroblocks similar to the VP8 video codec, which makes it poorly suited for these harsh borders.

When compressing high-contrast pages using lossy WebP, two primary problems occur:

  1. Ringing Artifacts and "Mosquito Noise": The mathematical transformation used in lossy compression cannot cleanly represent an instantaneous jump from pure black to pure white. This results in fuzzy gray smudges and ringing along the edges of ink lines, dialogue bubbles, and text.
  2. Screentone Degradation: Manga relies heavily on screentones (halftone patterns of tiny dots or fine hatching) to simulate gray shading. Lossy WebP reads these repeating micro-patterns as high-frequency noise, blurring them into muddy, inconsistent blotches or causing severe moiré patterns.
  3. Chroma Subsampling Issues: Even when an image is visually black and white, lossy WebP often converts data using YUV 4:2:0 color subsampling, which can introduce unnecessary color fringing if the source is not properly stripped to a true grayscale color space.

Why Lossless WebP Excels

Lossless WebP is the superior choice for high-contrast comics, as it uses advanced spatial prediction, entropy coding, and local color palette indexing.

  • Palette Optimization: Most black-and-white pages contain a limited color palette—often just two colors (1-bit binary) or a small range of grayscale anti-aliased edges. Lossless WebP identifies the restricted palette automatically, eliminating unnecessary data overhead.
  • Preservation of Fine Details: Because no information is discarded, razor-sharp ink lines, delicate cross-hatching, and micro-dot screentones remain pixel-perfect. There is zero blur, mosquito noise, or moiré distortion.
  • Superior Compression over PNG: Lossless WebP typically produces files that are 25% to 40% smaller than standard 8-bit or 24-bit PNG files while maintaining identical visual fidelity.

Best Practices for Encoding

To achieve optimal results when archiving or serving black-and-white comic pages via WebP:

  • Always Choose Lossless: Set the compression mode explicitly to lossless (-lossless in cwebp). The file size is often comparable to, or even smaller than, a lossy WebP encoded at a high-quality setting that still introduces artifacts.
  • Pre-process Grayscale: Ensure the source image is converted to grayscale or indexed color before encoding. Stripping out unused RGB channels prevents the encoder from allocating bits to redundant color data.
  • Avoid "Near-Lossless" for Screentones: WebP includes a "near-lossless" pre-processing filter designed to improve compression by slightly modifying pixel values. While useful for typical photography, it disrupts the strict geometry of screentone patterns and should be disabled for comics.
