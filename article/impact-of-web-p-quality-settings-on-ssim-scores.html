Impact of WebP Quality Settings on SSIM Scores

This article examines how adjusting WebP compression quality parameters directly influences Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) scores. By tracking the shift from low to high quality targets, we analyze the non-linear correlation between bitrate compression and perceived structural fidelity. Readers will understand the degradation patterns of WebP, how SSIM quantifies these losses, and where the mathematical "sweet spot" lies between visual retention and bandwidth efficiency.

The Relationship Between WebP Quality and SSIM

The WebP image format uses a quality parameter scaled from 0 to 100 to govern lossy compression. This value dictates quantization tables, macroblock prediction, and filtering strength. SSIM evaluates image fidelity by comparing luminance, contrast, and structural information between the original and compressed image, outputting a value from 0 to 1 (where 1 indicates identical structure).

As the WebP quality setting increases, SSIM scores follow a distinct logarithmic curve rather than a linear trajectory.

Quality Range 0 to 30: Severe Structural Loss

At the lowest end of the quality slider, the encoder aggressively discards high-frequency data and merges macroblocks:

  • SSIM Behavior: Scores typically fall between 0.70 and 0.85 depending on image complexity.
  • Visual Artifacts: Heavy blocking, ringing around edges, and severe blurring.
  • Impact: Structural data is compromised significantly to achieve maximum file size reduction.

Quality Range 31 to 75: The Efficiency Balance

This mid-tier range is where the encoder makes the most efficient trade-offs:

  • SSIM Behavior: Scores climb rapidly from roughly 0.86 to 0.95.
  • Visual Artifacts: Edge transitions are smoothed, but macroblocking is largely suppressed by the deblocking filter. Fine textures may lose sharpness, but primary subjects retain structural integrity.
  • Impact: Each point increase in quality yields a measurable jump in SSIM without causing exponential file size growth.

Quality Range 76 to 90: Diminishing Structural Returns

Most modern web delivery pipelines target this tier:

  • SSIM Behavior: SSIM scores stabilize between 0.96 and 0.985.
  • Visual Artifacts: Compression artifacts become nearly invisible to the naked eye under normal viewing conditions.
  • Impact: Moving past 80 provides marginal improvements in structural retention while disproportionately increasing byte size.

Quality Range 91 to 100: Near-Lossless Saturation

  • SSIM Behavior: SSIM approaches 0.99 to 1.0.
  • Visual Artifacts: None detectable under standard display conditions.
  • Impact: Increasing quality from 90 to 100 often doubles or triples the file size to capture microscopic high-frequency details that barely shift the structural score.

Key Takeaway for Optimization

SSIM demonstrates that WebP encoding hits a point of diminishing returns around the 75 to 82 quality range. In this zone, SSIM consistently rates above 0.95—the threshold generally accepted as visually lossless to human observers—while maintaining the drastic bandwidth savings that make the WebP format advantageous. Quality settings above 85 yield negligible structural gains relative to the data payload added.
