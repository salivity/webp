WebP vs HEIC Comparison for Smartphone Cameras

Modern smartphones capture high-resolution photos with complex depth data, requiring image formats that balance high fidelity with efficient storage. While Apple and Android manufacturers have adopted High Efficiency Image File Format (HEIC) as their default capture standard, Google’s WebP remains a dominant format for web delivery. This article compares WebP and native camera-generated HEIC files across image quality, compression, advanced camera features, hardware performance, and ecosystem compatibility to explain why smartphones rely on HEIC rather than WebP.

Compression Efficiency and Quality

HEIC, based on the HEVC (H.265) video compression standard, provides significantly higher compression efficiency for high-resolution photographs than WebP, which is derived from the older VP8 codec. In typical smartphone camera outputs (ranging from 12 to 200 megapixels), HEIC files are approximately 40% to 50% smaller than standard JPEGs while retaining superior edge definition and dynamic range.

WebP achieves roughly a 30% size reduction over JPEG, but its compression algorithms struggle more with high-frequency photographic noise, fine textures, and dynamic lighting gradients. Under aggressive compression, WebP tends to blur intricate details, whereas HEIC preserves textures more faithfully.

Bit Depth and Dynamic Range (HDR)

Modern smartphone sensors capture high dynamic range (HDR) scenes that exceed standard 8-bit color palettes. HEIC natively supports up to 16-bit color depth, allowing devices to store 10-bit HDR photos natively without banding or clipping highlights.

In contrast, standard lossy WebP is strictly an 8-bit format. Even though WebP lossless supports transparency and color profiles, it cannot natively handle the 10-bit or 12-bit sensor data required by modern HDR displays and processing pipelines. This makes standard WebP unsuitable as a native capture format for devices utilizing Smart HDR, Deep Fusion, or Ultra HDR.

Advanced Camera Features and Containers

HEIC is not merely an image format; it is a container format (based on ISOBMFF). This container structure allows smartphones to pack complex multi-frame data into a single file:

  • Live Photos and Motion Photos: HEIC stores both the still photo and the accompanying short video/audio clip in a single file.
  • Auxiliary Depth Maps: Smartphones store portrait mode depth maps, disparity data, and alpha masks directly within the HEIC container, enabling users to adjust background blur post-capture.
  • Burst Shots: Multiple full-resolution images from a single burst can be contained in one file.

WebP supports animation, but it lacks an extensible container framework capable of storing multiple distinct image streams, embedded depth metadata, and synchronised audio.

Hardware Encoding and Power Consumption

Capturing a photo on a smartphone requires instant encoding to keep the camera app responsive and conserve battery life. Smartphone System-on-Chips (SoCs)—including Apple's A-series, Qualcomm Snapdragon, and Google Tensor—contain dedicated hardware encoders for HEVC/HEIC.

Because the hardware is physically designed to process HEVC frames in real time, encoding HEIC takes milliseconds and draws minimal power. WebP lacks widespread dedicated hardware encoding blocks in mobile camera Image Signal Processors (ISPs), meaning encoding full-sensor WebP files would require software-based CPU or GPU processing, leading to higher latency and increased battery drain.

Compatibility and Ecosystem Support

Compatibility is the single area where WebP outperforms HEIC:

  • Web and Browsers: WebP is supported natively by virtually all modern web browsers, content management systems, and social networks, making it the preferred standard for online publishing.
  • Cross-Platform OS Support: HEIC works seamlessly within Apple and modern Android ecosystems, but it still encounters friction on older versions of Windows, Linux distributions, and web platforms that require software decoders or licensing fees.

Summary

Smartphones natively use HEIC instead of WebP because HEIC is purpose-built for the demands of modern digital photography. With support for 10-bit HDR, depth maps, multi-item image sequences, and dedicated hardware acceleration, HEIC remains the superior capture and storage format, while WebP remains best suited for lightweight web distribution.
