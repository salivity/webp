Lossy WebP Artifacts in Architectural Hatching

When fine architectural hatching is compressed using lossy WebP, the high-frequency linear patterns frequently trigger distinct edge artifacts, including ringing, block boundary fragmentation, and line bridging. Because lossy WebP relies on the VP8 video codec standard, its Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and spatial prediction algorithms struggle to preserve the sharp, high-contrast, repetitive transitions typical of technical drafting. This article outlines the specific edge degradation and visual distortions that occur when lossy WebP processes delicate hatch patterns.

Ringing Artifacts (Gibbs Phenomenon)

Ringing is the most prevalent artifact around dense architectural lines. When the WebP encoder quantizes high-frequency DCT coefficients to save data, it discards fine detail. As the decoder reconstructs the abrupt transition between a dark hatch line and a light background, ripples or "halos" appear parallel to the stroke edges. In dense parallel hatching, these ripples overlap, creating a cloudy, faint banding effect in the negative space between lines.

Block Boundary Discontinuities (Stepping and Shearing)

WebP operates on 16×16 macroblocks subdivided into 4×4 sub-blocks. Fine architectural lines that cross these block boundaries at shallow angles often break or shear. Instead of maintaining a continuous vector-like trajectory, the lines exhibit stair-stepping, micro-gaps, or abrupt pixel shifts at the block edges. This destroys the visual continuity critical for CAD elevations, section cuts, and material textures.

Line Bridging and Smearing

When hatching density approaches the pixel grid resolution, lossy WebP often fails to distinguish individual parallel lines. Quantization smooths out the valleys between peaks in the luminance channel, causing adjacent lines to fuse or "bridge." This transforms crisp cross-hatching into muddled, low-contrast smudges, particularly in dark or heavily textured areas like masonry or ground fills.

Deblocking Filter Distortion

WebP utilizes an adaptive in-loop deblocking filter designed to reduce visible seams between macroblocks. However, this filter cannot easily distinguish between artificial block edges and intentional, high-contrast hatch lines. When the filter attempts to smooth perceived edges, it softens and erodes fine line work, resulting in variable stroke thickness, localized blurring, and loss of line weight hierarchy across the drawing.

Directional Spatial Prediction Errors

The VP8 framework relies on intra-frame spatial prediction modes (such as horizontal, vertical, and DC prediction) to guess pixel values based on neighboring blocks. Dense diagonal hatching regularly conflicts with these cardinal prediction axes. When an incorrect prediction mode is selected, the encoder introduces directional smearing, ghost lines, or triangular artifacts along the boundaries of the macroblocks.
