WebP Chroma Subsampling and Saturated Reds

WebP manages compression efficiency through color space transformations that can impact high-frequency color detail, particularly with vibrant hues. While standard lossy WebP relies on 4:2:0 chroma subsampling—a mechanism notorious for causing pixelated bleeding and haloing around high-contrast saturated reds—the format mitigates these artifacts using specialized techniques like the "Sharp YUV" conversion filter and lossless RGB encoding. This article breaks down why saturated red edges degrade under standard WebP compression and the specific algorithms the format uses to eliminate or reduce these artifacts.

The Problem: 4:2:0 Subsampling on Red Boundaries

Lossy WebP is based on the VP8 video codec, which operates natively in the YUV 4:2:0 color space. In this space, the image is split into one full-resolution luma (brightness, Y) channel and two chroma (color, U and V) channels that are downsampled by half both horizontally and vertically.

The human visual system is far less sensitive to color detail than to brightness detail. However, this physiological assumption breaks down around saturated reds against dark or complementary (cyan, green) backgrounds. Saturated pure red has a relatively low luminance contribution compared to its chrominance intensity. When the chroma channel is downsampled using simple averaging filters (such as box filtering or bilinear downsampling), the boundary between the red object and its background is averaged across a \(2 \times 2\) pixel grid.

During decoding, the chroma plane is upsampled back to full resolution. Because the luminance edge remains sharp while the chroma edge is blurred, the red color "bleeds" past the physical edge of the object, creating dark fringes, noticeable ringing, or pixelated stair-stepping.

The Lossy Mitigation: Sharp YUV Conversion

To resolve red bleeding without abandoning the high compression ratios of 4:2:0, the official WebP encoder (libwebp) introduced an advanced preprocessing algorithm known as Sharp YUV (invoked via the -sharp_yuv flag).

Standard RGB-to-YUV downsampling processes pixels independently:

  1. Convert RGB pixels to full-resolution YUV.
  2. Downsample U and V channels using fixed mathematical averaging.

This feedforward approach does not check how the downsampled chroma will actually look once reconstructed.

In contrast, WebP's Sharp YUV uses an iterative, closed-loop optimization process:

  1. It creates an initial downsampled chroma map.
  2. It immediately simulates the decoder's upsampling process back to the original resolution.
  3. It compares the reconstructed RGB image against the original RGB source to measure spatial error.
  4. It iteratively adjusts the downsampled U and V values to minimize the difference between the reconstructed color boundary and the original sharp boundary.

By intentionally biasing the subsampled chroma values near edges, Sharp YUV compensates for the blur introduced during decoder upsampling. The resulting 4:2:0 lossy WebP image maintains clean, crisp boundaries on pure red text, logos, and high-contrast lines without increasing the file's dimensions or switching to an unsupported color profile.

The Absolute Solution: Lossless WebP

When artifacts cannot be tolerated—such as in pixel art, UI icons, or sharp vector graphics containing vibrant red elements—WebP bypasses chroma subsampling entirely through WebP Lossless.

Lossless WebP does not convert images into YUV 4:2:0. Instead, it processes data in the native ARGB color space (equivalent to 4:4:4 chroma sampling). Every individual pixel retains its full color resolution. The lossless format relies on spatial transformation algorithms, including:

  • Subtract Green Transform: Decorrelates red and blue values by subtracting the green channel from them, eliminating color redundancies without losing edge sharpness.
  • Spatial Predictors: Predicts pixel values using neighboring pixels, encoding only the difference (residual).
  • Color Indexing / Palette Transform: Identifies if only a few unique shades of red and background colors exist, mapping them directly to indexed values.

Because no spatial downsampling occurs on the color planes, lossless WebP completely eliminates chroma bleeding, color fringing, and edge blur around saturated reds.
